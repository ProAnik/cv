# Backend and cloud engineering application pitch

I am a software engineer working across backend services, AWS infrastructure, and production operations since 2021. My experience includes Python/FastAPI, C#/ASP.NET Core, PHP/Laravel, Terraform/Terragrunt infrastructure automation, and deploying and scaling MongoDB replica sets on EC2. In my current role, I build admissions CRM workflows and integrations, manage deployments and production support, and supervise development delivery. I am seeking a backend or cloud engineering role where I can contribute across application implementation, infrastructure, and reliability.

## How to use this draft

Use the Backend & Cloud CV for roles that match this experience. Keep official employment titles accurate; explain hands-on engineering scope directly underneath them. Use “Senior” in the application only where the vacancy and your actual responsibilities align.

For each application, put the two or three most relevant accomplishments first and match terminology to the vacancy when it accurately describes your experience.

Before submitting, check employment dates and current education status. The draft retains your existing CV's dates and facts. Client names and confidential work are omitted.

## Evidence you can discuss without identifying clients

Prepare three short examples: one production problem, one backend design decision, and one infrastructure or database change. For each, describe the general problem, your personal contribution, a tradeoff you considered, and the observed outcome. Use only information you are permitted to disclose. Do not invent metrics; a concrete qualitative result is useful.

## Finding where applications stall

If applications receive no screening calls, review role fit, CV evidence, and location or work-authorization requirements. If screening calls do not lead to technical interviews, refine how you explain your role and contribution. If technical interviews do not lead to offers, use interviewer feedback and practice the relevant problem-solving and system-design topics. These are diagnostic starting points, not guarantees.
